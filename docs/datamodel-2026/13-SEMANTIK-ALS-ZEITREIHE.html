Semantics as a time series

OpenTSx computes on numbers. ER1 is made of text. This document describes seven method families that turn meaning into series that can be evaluated numerically, and the one rule without which none of them yields a time series.

As of 2026-10-13 Subject ER1 → OpenTSx v2 Status concept, not implemented Related 04, 05, 11 · TASK-011 P8.1 · TASK-013

Why this is needed at all

The methods of OpenTSx are built for numerical series: StatsEngine computes moments and quantiles, DataQuality checks plausibility bands, the pattern search works with z-normalized Euclidean distance, and event synchronization compares event trains.

ER1, by contrast, carries titles, full text, categories and tags. None of it has an order on which a mean could be formed. If you point OpenTSx directly at ER1, you get count curves and nothing else: items per day, items per context. That is not wrong, but it is activity measurement and not an analysis of the content.

The gap is closed by a mapping that assigns to each item or each time window a number whose change means something. This mapping is the actual subject of the design. It is not neutral: it decides which questions can be answered later, and, if built wrongly, it can produce curves that measure the model instead of the corpus.

What this document does not propose

OpenTSx is not meant to handle semantics. Our semantic search exists separately as a local RAG and as a think-through pipeline, and our relationship graph as KafGraph. This document describes the third, complementary layer: derived numbers over the same source, joined through memory_id. Three layers, one key. None of them replaces the others.

The contract everything builds on

ER1 items exist as the Avro record Er1Item, registered in the Schema Registry as subject events.er1_items-value, version 1. That is the whole available surface. Anything not listed here has to be produced first.

The trap: tags is not a vocabulary field

Under the link convention of the memory layer, items also carry machine entries in tags of the form link/parent/<ctx>/<doc_id> and link/followup/<ctx>/<doc_id>. So the relationship graph sits in the same array as the content tags.

If you take tags unfiltered as a series dimension, you create a separate series per parent document. The cardinality then grows with the corpus instead of with the vocabulary. Splitting by prefix is mandatory: link/* becomes a graph edge, the rest becomes a dimension.

Where the series come from

The pipeline from ER1 to KafScale exists as code and is deployed. Nothing flows through it at the moment: the source topic holds zero records, and the unfolder has processed nothing since it started. The missing stage is the source connector, not the evaluation.

The rule before the methods

A semantic measure only becomes a time series when the mapping is frozen and versioned. Otherwise the curve measures the model and not the corpus, and no statement about it can be checked.

1 · Pin down the mapping

The model, chunking, reference vectors and window width belong in the provenance of the message. opentsx.vector.v1 has the field provenance for this. For the corpus, corpus_hash and git_commit of the index are the fitting values.

2 · Always a control series

At least one measure must work without a model. Only then can model drift be told apart from real change later. Without this series, every interpretation is unfalsifiable. Family E supplies candidates.

3 · A change of method is a new series

A changed mapping does not correct values, it produces different ones. Because OpenTSx buckets carry a manifest and a checksum and can be replayed, the interpretation can be versioned and both variants kept side by side. That is the main argument for putting this into OpenTSx and not into a script.

The same in the language of the event taxonomy: the event system transports state changes, it does not decide them. A metrics pipeline that classifies does decide. See family G.

4 · The control series is model-free, not channel-free

Rule 2 rules out the model as a cause, not the recording channel. A dictaphone field note has a different vocabulary than a coordination item. On a channel change, the model-dependent and the model-free series therefore swing out alike and confirm each other, without a change of subject having to be involved. A third control is needed, stratified by the declared channel, for example content_type.

This rule came from a measurement and not from a principle. On 2026-10-13, rule 2 was met over 20 adjacent pairs of weeks, drift against the control series +0.514 at p=0.020. The three largest drift peaks visibly coincided with a channel change, but over all pairs this could be neither confirmed nor ruled out: drift against channel change +0.272 at p=0.246. The cross-check within one channel had too little statistical power, because only one channel fills enough weeks and its own noise floor there lies above the overall drift. Open, and therefore noted as a rule rather than a finding.

The seven method families

The letters are names, not an order. Two badges per family encode the two axes that matter technically: whether a measure depends on a model, and whether it is produced per item or per time window. The first axis determines reproducibility, the second the type of time axis, irregular versus regular.

A

Anchor projections

model-dependentper item

You choose k fixed reference texts, one per subject, and embed them once. For each item, the cosine similarity to each anchor is computed. One item becomes k numbers, and the stream becomes k parallel series that stay comparable over years, because the anchors do not move.

a_j = anchor_j / |anchor_j| j = 1..k, once v_i = embed(text_i) s_ij = (v_i · a_j) / (|v_i| · |a_j|) one value per item per anchor
Input
text, event_ts
Output
k scalars per item, value range about [-1, 1]
Question
How strongly did subject X fill this period, and when does the ratio between two subjects tip over?
Weakness
You see only what you anchor. The choice of anchors is a decision about content and must be documented.

Mapping The most direct fit in the existing model. The k values of a tick are exactly one opentsx.vector.v1: components are the anchor names in a fixed order, values the cosine values, and provenance carries the model and the corpus hash. Produced by the operator align.vector from TASK-013, written to tsx.<domain>.smart.<name>.vectors.v1.

B

Novelty and drift

model-dependentper item

Distance of an item to the centroid of the preceding window. High values mean: something appears here that did not appear in the recent past. At the window level there is the variant centroid step: how far the center of the corpus moves from one window to the next.

c_t = mean( v_i : i in window W(t) ) novelty_i = 1 - cos( v_i , c_{t-1} ) drift_t = 1 - cos( c_t , c_{t-1} ) one number per window
Input
text, event_ts, window width
Output
one scalar per item, plus one per window
Question
When is a subject new, and how fast does the center shift?
Weakness
Relative to its own past, so not comparable across years. Answers a different question than A, not the same one better.

Mapping Novelty as an irregular Observation per memory_id, drift as a regular series with the window width as clock.

C

Spread and effective dimensionality

model-dependentper window

Per time window, the mean pairwise distance, which is focus versus fragmentation. Sharper through the eigenvalue spectrum of the covariance in the window: the participation ratio says how many distinguishable directions were active at the same time. This makes a quantity measurable that you otherwise only have as a feeling.

spread_t = mean( 1 - cos(v_i, v_j) : i < j in window ) PR_t = ( sum_m lambda_m )^2 / sum_m lambda_m^2 lambda_m = eigenvalues of the covariance in the window
Input
all v_i of a window
Output
two scalars per window, PR roughly in [1, k]
Question
Was this week focused or frayed, and on how many subjects at the same time?
Weakness
The most sensitive to the choice of window and the window occupancy. With few items per window, PR is practically noise. Set a minimum occupancy and record it alongside.

Mapping Regular series, window as a half-open interval, missing counts underfilled ticks. The minimum occupancy belongs in provenance.

D

Channel occupancy

model-free with declared channelsper window

Each channel of a fixed channel set gets a count series: items per channel per tick. This yields the multivariate event trains that event synchronization needs. This family is the bridge from meaning to questions about dependency and lag.

The subject of the design is not the method but where the channels come from. Clustering over embeddings is the worst of the available options for this, because it draws the axes from the data and so creates the trap in the next section in the first place. Declared channel sets do not have this problem, and we have two of them.

chan(i) from a DECLARED set, not from clustering n_mt = | { i : chan(i) = m , event_ts_i in tick t } | n_?t = | { i : chan(i) undefined , event_ts_i in tick t } | mandatory channel
Input
channel set with a validity interval per channel, event_ts
Output
one count series per channel, plus the channel "unassigned"
Question
Do two channels run in sync, or does one follow the other with a lag?
Weakness
With clustering: re-clustering shifts the axes retroactively, see the figure below. With declared channels: a channel that did not exist yet is not zero but absent. If you confuse the two, you count project starts as a drop.

Mapping One regular series per channel, combined as one opentsx.vector.v1 with the cluster names as components. The consumer is ESCalc or event_sync.py, with local τ after Quiroga. The Q_rand calibration and the event coincidence analysis from P8.1 give such statements exactly the reliability they need.

E

Lexical and information-theoretic

model-freeper window

Rate of change directly on the term distributions, without any model. The Jensen-Shannon distance between consecutive windows is the classic reliable measure for a change of subject. In addition: the rate of new terms, the type-token ratio, the hapax share and the surprisal of a window under a model that was estimated only on the previous windows.

P_t = relative term frequencies in window t JS(P_t, P_{t-1}) = 0.5 * KL(P_t || M) + 0.5 * KL(P_{t-1} || M) M = 0.5 * (P_t + P_{t-1}) newterm_t = | terms in t, never seen before | / | terms in t |
Input
title, text, event_ts
Output
several scalars per window, JS in [0, 1]
Question
How much has the vocabulary changed, independent of any model?
Weakness
Shallow: it does not see synonyms and rephrasings. Language-dependent in tokenization, so record the tokenizer version alongside. And the bias in sparse windows is not theoretical: measured on the corpus, the rank correlation between token mass and JS is −0.79 over 29 months. Without matching the token mass, this series mainly measures how much was written.

Mapping Regular series. This is the control series from rule 2. Deterministic, cheap, auditable. Carry at least one of them along, even if A to D look nicer.

F

Graph-derived measures

model-freeper item

Because tags carries the typed links and KafGraph already materializes the edges, structural quantities can be measured per interval: out-degree, number of components, depth of the longest chain, share of follow-ups versus root items.

The most valuable of them is a latency: the time between an item and the first item that refers to it. This is a real duration, needs no semantic model and still concerns content, because it measures how fast there is a reaction to what was written down.

The edge kind decides, and it is not the one the name promises. Measured on the corpus on 2026-10-13, 5111 items over 30 months:

Edge kindEdgesSame dayMedianp90Usable as a duration
link/parent87478 %07no, comment edge
link/continues15999 %00no, session continuation
link/followup7596 %00no, despite the name
link/step11011 %725yes
link/gegenfrage359 %925yes
link/artifact5433 %215partly

link/parent is a comment edge: child and parent are created in the same session, the latency there is structurally zero and says nothing about action. Despite its name, link/followup carries no latency, 96 percent on the same day. The duration is in link/step and link/gegenfrage.

Addendum 2026-10-13: the two medians of 7 and 9 days are biased downward. The duration edges do not exist over 30 months but only since 2026-08: 31 edges in 2026-08, 97 in 2026-09, 17 up to 2026-10-13. Taken month by month, the medians rise monotonically, for link/step from 0.53 through 7.25 to 14.65 days and for link/gegenfrage from 0.06 through 7.92 to 19.07.

This is not a change in behavior but censoring at the start. In the first month of a mechanism no long latency can be observed, because there are no old targets; the observable duration is capped by the age of the mechanism. This gives a rule of thumb for the whole family: the observation window must be longer than the duration that is to be estimated. For link/step with a p90 around 25 days, two months are enough, and that is met. The p90 is itself censored and can still rise with age: pooled over the three months it is 25.1 days, and in September alone it was 198 days for link/gegenfrage.

EDGE = link/<kind>/<ctx>/<doc_id>, kind from {step, gegenfrage} latency_e = event_ts_child - event_ts_target per EDGE, not per item depth_t = longest chain that ends in tick t synced-from is sync machinery and is excluded
Input
tags with link/step or link/gegenfrage, event_ts
Output
duration per edge, structural quantities per window
Question
How fast does a note turn into an action, and is the backlog growing?
Weakness
Censored, and the denominator is not the item count: most items are never meant to have a child. A rate "share with a child" over all items is meaningless. The evaluation runs per edge kind over the edges themselves. In addition, n is small, 110 and 35 in the measured corpus, which supports a median, not a weekly series.

Mapping Latency as an irregular Observation with unit s, structural quantities as regular series. The only family that needs neither embeddings nor full text.

G

Rubric scores

model-dependentper item

Rate each item against a fixed rubric, one value between 0 and 1 per dimension: decision versus observation, blocked versus flowing, internal versus customer. Each rubric dimension becomes a series. This is the only family that makes concepts measurable for which there is no anchor text.

What matters is where the rating takes place. Not in the metrics pipeline. A metrics job that asks a model itself produces different numbers on every run and cannot be replayed. The rating belongs in the reviewer stage of the event taxonomy and leaves it as an event of its own, classification.proposed, with its own provenance. Family G then consumes this event instead of calling a model.

WRONG: metrics job -> model -> value new on every run RIGHT: reviewer -> classification.proposed -> metrics job -> value r_id = from classification.proposed, not recomputed agree_d = spread from repeated rating of the same items
Input
classification.proposed with the rubric version and the model version in its provenance
Output
D scalars per item, plus an agreement measure
Question
How does the character of the work shift, not its subject?
Weakness
Remains the most expensive family: running cost per item, and without repeated rating the uncertainty is unknown. Under rule 3, a rubric change is a new series, not an update.

Mapping Like A, one opentsx.vector.v1; components are the rubric dimensions. The gain of the event variant is that the series lies below a decision record: the classification is recorded once with its provenance and is not reinvented on every metrics run. This makes G as reproducible as A, although a model was involved.

Three channel sets, three questions

Family D stands or falls with the channel set. There is not one right set but three, which answer different questions. Two of them are already declared in our setup and so are superior to clustering.

Channel setOriginAnswersNatural measure
PLM projects declared, managed externally. The binding exists through the project:<id> tags and the project descriptor. Which work got attention, and is the ratio shifting? Items per project per tick
Lifecycle events declared. The taxonomy intake.created, classification.proposed, operation.permit, work.created, verification.completed and the others. Where does work get stuck, and is the jam growing? Dwell time per state, transition rate, number of stuck items
Embedding clusters discovered, not declared. What am I working on that no declared category covers? Distance to the nearest declared channel

The PLM projects are the right primary channel set. They settle the freezing question completely, because their identity is managed outside the measurement: a project has the same name tomorrow as today, regardless of what happens in the corpus. Rule 3 thereby stops being a risk, and the channels are interpretable by design. A cluster name like c7 has to be interpreted first, a project name does not.

Three things must be kept clean here. First, a mandatory channel "unassigned" is needed, otherwise mass disappears unnoticed; its size is itself a useful measure, namely how much work runs outside any project structure. Second, projects are not disjoint: an item can carry two. Either assign it proportionally or count it in both; if you count it in both, the channel sum exceeds the item count, and that must be documented. Third, projects have a validity interval. Before the start, the channel is absent, not zero.

The lifecycle events are the second declared set, and they answer the more valuable question. Projects say where attention was; states say where it got stuck. The natural measures there are not counts but dwell times, and so the same censored data type as the follow-up latency from family F: an item that never left a state does not have an infinite dwell time but one that is not yet known.

Clustering stays in the plan, but with its task reversed: not as a channel set but as an outlier detector against the declared taxonomy. Items that lie far from every project and every state are exactly the interesting ones, because they show what the existing structure does not capture. This is more useful than clustering for its own sake, and it avoids the axis trap, because no historical value depends on the clustering.

Measured on 2026-10-13: the project binding is younger and thinner than the corpus

Out of 5111 items, 629 carry a project: tag, that is 12.3 percent, and only from 2026-05 on; before that lie 1529 items without any project binding. link/project occurs three times. So the binding runs through context_id and the export manifest, not through tags.

The checked export holds two contexts and one PLM project, and one of the two contexts appears practically only as a one-time bulk import in a single month. The channel dimension is thus degenerate on this export. The prerequisite for family D is therefore one export per PLM project, each into its own folder with its own descriptor. Without this step there is one channel, not a channel set.

Side effect for family A

If the channels are projects, the anchors can also come from the project descriptions. Then A and D share one taxonomy, and the anchor projection becomes directly readable as "closeness to project X", without having to invent subjects separately. The choice of anchors, named explicitly as a weakness under A, thereby disappears as a free decision.

How wide is a bucket

The window width is not a question of presentation; it decides whether the measure is estimable. And it is not the same for all families.

E and C: ISO week

Three reasons, and all three point in the same direction. First, estimability: the Jensen-Shannon distance between two term distributions is biased upward with few tokens per window, because sampling noise looks like change. The smaller the window, the larger the spurious change. Second, the weekly rhythm: with daily windows, the difference between workday and weekend creates a 7-day period that masks any real trend and itself looks like signal. Weekly windows remove it by design. Third, readability: the week is already our reporting unit, so every swing can be checked against known events.

D: day or finer

For event synchronization, the window is not the aggregation interval but the coincidence width τ. Counted by week, every simultaneity is trivially met and the method says nothing anymore. So the count channels need daily resolution or finer, and τ is chosen separately and deliberately, locally after Quiroga instead of globally.

What must be recorded alongside

JS values are comparable only between windows of similar token mass. The token count per window therefore belongs as a component of its own in the same opentsx.vector.v1, not in a footnote. Without it, it cannot be decided later whether a swing was content or a sparse window. missing counts underfilled ticks.

How one text becomes k series

Family A as a picture. What matters is that the anchors are fixed before the first measurement and not touched afterwards. Then every single value is comparable with every later one.

The trap in family D

Clustering is tempting, because it needs no choice of anchors. The price is that the axes come from the data. If you re-cluster, you change the meaning of values already written, without any number visibly moving.

What comes first

Not all seven. The order follows the dependencies, not the appeal: first the three model-free families that need only timestamps and tags, then the first content family, then the sensitive ones. With PLM projects as the channel set, D is the cheapest of all and no longer the fourth.

Before all of that: measure, do not build

As long as er1a.events.er1_items is empty, any series modeling is speculation. The first step is not family A but restoring the source connector and then reading the topic once from the start: items per day per ctx_id, per category, per source and per non-link/ tag. Read-only, no new topics. Only these cardinalities tell what the series key must look like.

A caveat about the existing index

The embeddings for families A, B, C and D do not have to be obtained anew. They already exist, local and offline, in the RAG index of the notes corpus.

PropertyValueMeaning for metrics
modelBAAI/bge-m3multilingual, covers German and English notes together
dim1024dense, suitable for cosine measures
bit_width4the caveat. Quantization noise goes straight into every cosine distance
chunks98,677enough for frozen clustering
corpus_hash, git_commitsetexactly the anchor that rule 1 requires for provenance

The 4-bit quantization is sized correctly for retrieval, where recall is what counts. For metrics it is not necessarily so: the noise lands directly in the curves from A, B and C.

Two ways. Either recompute the embeddings in full precision for exactly the items that become series. Or accept the noise and measure it, by computing a subset twice and documenting the difference as a noise band in the series. For a start, the second way is the better one, because it is cheap and first answers the question whether the difference matters at all.

What this document does not claim

  • As of 2026-10-13, outdated: five of the seven families have since been implemented and run over the ER1 corpus. A, B, C, E and F exist as tools in a private notes repository, family D as a cross-section. Still open are the second D variant with dwell times, which lacks the state transitions in the export, and family G, which lacks the reviewer stage.
  • Outdated: the latency table in family F and the numbers in the fourth rule come from runs over 5119 ER1 items. Everything else in this document is still specification and not measurement. Where a value is measured, its date is given with it.
  • Answered, and mostly in the negative. Over eight months and 25 ISO weeks, none of the nine anchor series shows a trend, and drift, spread and participation ratio show none either. Anchor projections only hold up after centering per item: 95.9 % of what raw values measure is a common factor, namely the text length. One finding is established, namely that drift and spread are uncorrelated, so the cloud shifts and does not widen.
  • The description of the ER1 memory layer itself, that is doc_id, contexts and the link convention, comes from the tool descriptions and is not checked against the source code of the ER1 backend. The Avro contract, by contrast, is read from the running Schema Registry.
  • The event taxonomy in family G and in the channel section is taken from a draft note, not from a specification. It is not implemented anywhere yet, and the names can change. What remains is the split: classifying belongs in a stage of its own with an event of its own, not in the metrics pipeline.
  • For the term "JEV" named in this context, there is no definition in the corpus. Three occurrences are on record: a draft note from 2026-09-22 with the wording "ER1 → Kafka/KafScale → JEV-like classification → topics", and two YouTube items from 2026-09-28 that carry the tag. Their transcript describes a cheap, fast decision model whose decision is read off from the layers instead of being generated. The automatic subtitles spell the same spoken name alternately "Laya" and "LLaMA", so the spelling in the corpus is not reliable evidence. Family G is therefore still described by the requirement, namely an externally defined, verifiable classification stage, and not by a particular method.
